Section 01

Architecture principles

Seven decisions that everything else follows from. If a future page conflicts with one of these, the page is wrong, not the principle.

#PrincipleWhat it means in practice
1One term, one canonical pageEvery owned term has exactly one page that defines it. No term is defined in two places. Everything else links to that page.
2Four pillars, not nineAuthority consolidates. Four deep pillars outrank nine shallow ones. Additional topics become cluster pages, not new pillars.
3Two clicks maximumNo page a CEO needs is more than two clicks from the homepage.
4One job, one CTA per pageEach page does one of: define, diagnose, apply, prove, convert. Each drives exactly one action.
5Commercial pages receive, they do not distributeSpeaking, Facilitator Certification, and Advisory absorb links from framework content. They do not link to each other.
6The firm is the entity, not the founderBlue Lake Ideas owns the terms, the schema, and the citations. Matt Elliott appears as founder and author inside that entity.
7Every page carries a machine layerVisible FAQs plus matching JSON-LD on every substantive page. AI answer engines cite what they can extract.
Why this structure

Most consultant sites organize around services. That structure sells to someone who has already decided. Blue Lake Ideas sells to a CEO who has a problem they have not yet named. The architecture leads with the problem and the vocabulary.

SEO title pattern

Owned term front-loaded, under 60 characters, brand suffix only when room allows. Example: & Intelligence™: The Practice for Leading Under Load. Never lead with "Blue Lake Ideas."

Meta description pattern

150 to 160 characters. Owned term in the first eight words. One benefit clause. No exclamation marks, no ellipses.

Why the audit is the header CTA

"Bring Matt In" asks a stranger to commit before they have a reason to. The audit asks a question the reader wants answered about themselves, takes four minutes, and delivers a qualified record. It works equally for cold search visitors and post-keynote visitors.

Footer navigation

Four columns: The system (all five framework pages) · Work with us (Speaking, Certification, Advisory) · More (Overloaded, Insights, About, Connect) · Follow (LinkedIn, YouTube, Substack). Legal and trademark line beneath.

Breadcrumbs

Required on every Level 2 page, with BreadcrumbList schema. Example: Home → & Intelligence™ → Find the &.

Section 07

Internal linking law

Five rules. Non-negotiable. This is the difference between a website and a topic cluster.

#RuleInstruction
1Cluster links up, alwaysEvery cluster page links to its pillar at least once, using the exact owned term as anchor text. Not "read more," not "learn about the framework."
2Pillar links down, alwaysEvery pillar links out to each of its cluster pages. A cluster page with no inbound link from its pillar is orphaned and will not rank.
3Never cross-link cluster to clusterRoute through the pillar. Cross-cluster links dilute which page owns which term.
4First mention links, later mentions do notThe first appearance of any owned term on a page links to its canonical definition. Subsequent appearances are plain text.
5Commercial pages receive, they do not distributeSpeaking, Certification, and Advisory absorb links from framework content. They link back to pillars but never sideways to each other.

The link audit test

Open any published page at random. Can you reach the correct canonical definition of every owned term it uses in one click? If not, the page is not finished. Run this test monthly.

Heading hierarchyOne H1 per page containing the owned term. H2s follow section order in the brief. No skipped levels.
PerformanceLargest Contentful Paint under 2.5 seconds on mobile. Executives read on phones between meetings.
XML sitemapAuto-generated, submitted, excludes conversion form pages and audit result variants.
Why the machine layer matters more than usual here

Blue Lake Ideas is trying to own vocabulary that does not yet exist in the market. AI answer engines resolve unfamiliar terms by finding a source that defines them cleanly and consistently. Visible FAQs with matching schema, one canonical page per term, and consistent anchor text are the three signals that decide whether Blue Lake Ideas gets cited as the definition.

Section 12

Governance

Architecture decays without a rule for what happens next.

When someone proposes a new page

  • Which of the four pillars does it report to? If none, it is not a page yet.
  • Which owned term does it own or support?
  • What is its one job and its one CTA?

When someone proposes a fifth pillar

The answer is almost always no. Four pillars concentrate authority. New topics become cluster pages, or sub-pillars if they genuinely require indexed depth.

After every publish

  • Record the URL and publish date against the term in the Command Center
  • Add the inbound link from the parent pillar — a cluster page without one is orphaned
  • Run the link audit test on the new page before marking it complete
The final test

Could a search engine, an AI answer engine, a keynote audience member, a board chair, and an advisory client each encounter the same idea in a different place and still recognize it as the same Blue Lake Ideas system? If yes, the architecture is holding.
