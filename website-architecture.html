Section 01

Architecture principles

Seven decisions that everything else follows from. They implement the buyer chain in Section 02. If a future page conflicts with one of these, the page is wrong, not the principle.

#PrincipleWhat it means in practice
1One term, one canonical pageEvery owned term has exactly one page that defines it. No term is defined in two places. Everything else links to that page.
2The structure mirrors the chainProblem, Cost, Solution, Method, Tools, Outcome each get a home in that order. Need lives in the hero. Four deep pillars carry it, because authority consolidates and four outrank nine.
3Two clicks maximumNo page a CEO needs is more than two clicks from the homepage. Depth of content, not depth of navigation.
4One job, one CTA per pageEach page does one of: define, diagnose, apply, prove, convert. Each drives exactly one action.
5Commercial pages receive, they do not distributeSpeaking, Facilitator Certification, and Advisory absorb links from framework content. They do not link to each other. Keep the exit clean.
6The firm is the entity, not the founderBlue Lake Ideas owns the terms, the schema, and the citations. Matt Elliott appears as founder and author inside that entity. Search and AI engines attach owned vocabulary to whichever entity the markup names.
7Every page carries a machine layerVisible FAQs plus matching JSON-LD on every substantive page. AI answer engines cite what they can extract.
Why this structure and not a conventional one

Most consultant sites organize around services: speaking, consulting, book, about. That structure sells to someone who has already decided. Blue Lake Ideas sells to a CEO who has a problem they have not yet named. So the architecture leads with the problem and the vocabulary, and treats the services as three depths of one system rather than three products in a catalog.

Section 03

Where the architecture deviates, and why

The chain and the site structure agree almost everywhere. Seven places they do not. Each is either a resolved conflict that needs a decision, or a deliberate deviation for inbound reasons.

01

The three steps changed owners twice

Needs a decision

The conflict. An earlier direction placed the three steps under The Power of &™ as its framework steps. Matt's chain places them under the & Intelligence™ Framework as the Method, with The Power of &™ standing alone as the Solution.

What this build does. Follows Matt. The steps live at /and-intelligence/* and & Intelligence™ becomes the deep pillar at 2,000 to 2,500 words. The Power of &™ drops to 1,200 words as the belief-level Solution page.

Why it matters. This is the single highest-consequence decision in the architecture. It sets which page is deepest, which URL the steps live under, and which term the anchor text points to. Reversing it later means redirects, rewrites, and lost authority. Confirm it once, in writing, before the copywriter starts.

02

Workshops or Facilitator Certification

Needs a decision

The conflict. Matt's Tools layer lists Workshops. The current build has & Intelligence™ Facilitator Certification, a credential that qualifies others to teach the framework.

The distinction. A workshop is delivered to a team. A certification is earned by a facilitator. They are different products, different buyers, and different price points. They can both exist, but they cannot share a page.

Recommendation. Keep both. Workshops become the Tools-layer delivery at /workshops. Certification stays nested under the method as the scaling play. If only one ships first, ship Workshops: it is the shorter sales cycle.

03

The Need layer has no page, on purpose

Deliberate

The deviation. "They need to execute and transform" is the top of the chain but gets no pillar.

Why. Nobody searches for their own need in those words. They search the symptom or the cost. A page targeting "execute and transform" competes with every consultancy on earth and wins none of it. The Need belongs in the hero, where it establishes recognition in one line, and in the Capacity Advantage page, where it becomes the payoff.

04

Cost is a sub-pillar, not a pillar

Deliberate

The deviation. The chain gives Cost equal weight to Problem. The architecture nests The Four Taxes under The Binary Trap.

Why. HubSpot's topic cluster model concentrates authority. Four deep pillars outrank five shallow ones, and the taxes are the consequence of the trap rather than a separate topic. The Four Taxes still gets its own indexed URL, its own FAQ set, and the highest conversion intent of any framework page. It loses nothing but the pillar label.

05

Tools are ordered by buyer readiness, not by value

Deliberate

The deviation. Matt lists Advisory first. The site surfaces Books and Keynotes earlier and holds Advisory for later in the journey.

Why. Advisory is the highest-value and highest-commitment tool. Inbound sequencing puts the lowest-friction offer in front of a cold visitor and escalates. Advisory still receives links from every pillar, and it remains the deepest page in the funnel. Order on the page is not a statement about importance.

06

Books, plural

Confirm

The question. The chain says Books. The build has one, Overloaded.

Handling. The URL is /overloaded, not /book, which means a second title slots in later without a migration. If more are planned, say so now and the section becomes /books with children.

07

Is System 1 part of the term

Confirm

The question. Matt writes "the System 1 Binary Trap." The build treats System 1 as the mechanism inside the trap, not part of the name.

Recommendation. Keep the term short. "The Binary Trap" is what an audience repeats and what someone types into a search bar. System 1 explains why it happens and belongs in the body copy, with attribution to Kahneman.

Open discrepancy flagged by the build team

Finding 01 sets & Intelligence™ at 2,000 to 2,500 words and The Power of &™ at 1,200. Some earlier page-directory drafts still carry the reverse. The Section 03 decision governs. Every word count in the Page Directory below reflects it.

SEO title pattern

Owned term front-loaded, under 60 characters, brand suffix only when room allows. Example: & Intelligence™: The Method for Leading Under Load. Never lead with "Blue Lake Ideas."

Meta description pattern

150 to 160 characters. Owned term in the first eight words. One benefit clause. No exclamation marks, no ellipses.

Why the audit is the header CTA

"Bring Matt In" asks a stranger to commit before they have a reason to, and it makes the firm look like one person's calendar. The audit asks a question the reader wants answered about themselves, takes four minutes, and delivers a qualified record. It works equally for a cold search visitor and a post-keynote visitor.

Section 08

Internal linking law

This is the difference between a website and a topic cluster. Five rules, no exceptions.

#RuleInstruction
1Cluster links up, alwaysEvery cluster page links to its pillar at least once in the body, using the exact owned term as anchor text. Not "read more."
2Pillar links down, alwaysEvery pillar links out to each of its cluster pages. A cluster page with no inbound link from its pillar is orphaned and will not rank.
3Never cross-link cluster to clusterRoute through the pillar. Cross-cluster links dilute which page owns which term.
4First mention links, later mentions do notThe first appearance of any owned term on a page links to its canonical definition. Subsequent appearances are plain text.
5Commercial pages receive, they do not distributeSpeaking, Workshops, Certification, and Advisory absorb links from framework and proof content. They link back up to pillars but never sideways to each other.

The link audit test

Open any published page at random. Can you reach the correct canonical definition of every owned term it uses in one click? If not, the page is not finished. Run this test monthly across the whole site.

Why the machine layer matters more than usual here

Blue Lake Ideas is trying to own vocabulary that does not yet exist in the market. AI answer engines resolve unfamiliar terms by finding a source that defines them cleanly and consistently. Visible FAQs with matching schema, one canonical page per term, and consistent anchor text are the three signals that decide whether Blue Lake Ideas gets cited as the definition or gets paraphrased into someone else's.

Section 13

Governance

Architecture decays without a rule for what happens next.

When someone proposes a new page

  • Which layer of the buyer chain does it serve? If none, it is not a page yet. It is an idea looking for a home.
  • Which of the four pillars does it report to?
  • What is its one job and its one CTA? If you cannot answer both in one sentence each, the page is not scoped.

When someone proposes a fifth pillar

The answer is almost always no. Four pillars concentrate authority. A fifth splits it. New topics become cluster pages under an existing pillar, or sub-pillars if they genuinely require their own indexed depth, which so far only The Four Taxes has.

After every publish

  • Record the URL and publish date against the term in the Command Center
  • Add the inbound link from the parent pillar. A cluster page without one is orphaned
  • Run the link audit test on the new page before marking it complete
The final test

Could a search engine, an AI answer engine, a keynote audience member, a board chair, and an advisory client each encounter the same idea in a different place and still recognize it as the same Blue Lake Ideas system? If yes, the architecture is holding.

Section 14

Inbound flywheel map

HubSpot's methodology replaced the funnel with a flywheel: attract, engage, delight, with satisfied customers feeding the next turn. Matt's chain maps onto it cleanly, which is the strongest argument that the chain is right.

StageChain layers it servesPages doing the workWhat good looks like
AttractNeed, Problem, CostHomepage, Binary Trap, Four Taxes, Insights problem clusterA CEO who could not name the pattern finds the words for it. Measured by pillar sessions and organic entry on the problem cluster.
EngageSolution, Method, ToolsPower of &, & Intelligence and its three steps, Capacity Audit, Keynotes, Workshops, AdvisoryThey trade an email for something useful, then escalate. Measured by audit completion and MQL to SQL conversion.
DelightOutcomeCapacity Advantage, proof cases, Certification, book, nurtureThey use the language with their own team, and it comes back as referrals and repeat engagements. Measured by source of first touch on closed work.

Where the flywheel changes the build

  • Friction is the enemy, not the funnel stage. Every form field that is not used for routing or qualification gets deleted. The chapter one capture takes an email and nothing else.
  • Delight feeds Attract. Certified facilitators and past clients repeating the owned language is the highest-quality attraction the firm has. That is the strategic argument for the certification, independent of its revenue.
  • Content answers questions, it does not describe services. Each pillar answers the question a buyer actually asks at that layer of the chain, which is why the page directory records a reader question for every page.
  • The Capacity Audit is the flywheel's bearing. It converts Attract traffic into Engage records and gives Delight a personalized starting point. Build it before any additional article.
The alignment check

Matt's chain and the inbound flywheel are the same shape: name the problem before selling the answer, escalate commitment gradually, and let the outcome generate the next visitor. Where the architecture deviates from the chain, Section 03 says so and gives the reason. Nothing else in this document departs from either.
