01Read this first

Blue Lake Ideas is not a collection of pages. It is one intellectual system. Your job is to make every asset recognizable as part of the same system.

The spine every asset sits on

They need to execute and transform. Accelerating change → overload → the mind narrows → interdependent priorities get recast as either/or → the organization pays. That is The Binary Trap, and the price is The Four Taxes. The solution is The Power of &™: hold the tension instead of resolving it. The & Intelligence™ Framework is how you bring it to life, in three steps. The result is The Capacity Advantage.

Five rules that govern every draft

RuleWhat it means when you are writing
One job per assetDEFINE, DIAGNOSE, APPLY, PROVE, or CONVERT. Do not let a page do two.
One CTA per assetExactly one. Secondary routes may appear as plain text links, never as a second button.
Lead with the pointNo setup, no "in today's fast-paced world." The first sentence is the strongest sentence.
One mantra per assetBefore you submit, underline the one sentence a CEO should still remember tomorrow.
No em dashesClient standard. Use a period, a comma, or a colon.
The firm owns the systemBlue Lake Ideas owns the framework, the language, and the practice. Matt Elliott is the founder and principal voice.
The reader

A CEO or board member who is already overloaded. They will give you ninety seconds and they are reading on a phone between meetings. Respect their capacity on the surface. Give search engines and AI answer engines the depth underneath.

Banned on sight

Unlock. Transform. Bespoke. Holistic. Leverage as a verb. "In today's fast-paced world." "Bring your whole self." Any consulting phrase that could appear on any other firm's website.

Trademark handling

™ on first use per page for The Power of &™ and & Intelligence™. Not on subsequent mentions. Use the ampersand character, never the word "and," in either term.

04Foundation
A1 · Wave 1

Homepage

bluelakeideas.com
Content job
Diagnose Teach the system before selling the services.
Reader question
What is this and is it for someone in my chair?
Length
1,000–1,400 words. Modular and highly scannable. Assume phone.
Required terms
The Binary Trap · The Four Taxes · The Power of &™ · & Intelligence™ · The Capacity Advantage · Clarity. Connection. Courage.
Section order
  1. Above the fold: concession, pivot, named mechanism, what the firm does, one CTA.
  2. Recognition: accelerating change and the always-on AI era consume capacity.
  3. Mechanism: name The Binary Trap and walk the chain in one clean sequence.
  4. Cost: introduce all Four Taxes with one-sentence definitions each.
  5. Bridge: the answer is not to eliminate tension — it is to build capacity to hold it.
  6. The framework: The Power of &™ in one line, then three steps with their outcomes.
  7. Payoff: Clarity. Connection. Courage. leading to The Capacity Advantage.
  8. Applications: Speaking, Workshops, Advisory, Overloaded as four depths of one system.
  9. Proof: practitioner credibility plus approved institutional proof.
  10. FAQ: three questions, complete sentences.
  11. Final CTA.
The hook
The reader is not unaware. They are unarticulated. A CEO arriving here already knows their capacity is gone. What they cannot do is explain why that loss keeps producing bad either/or decisions. Write copy that hands them the language for a problem they are already living.
One CTA
Explore & Intelligence™. Secondary routes as text links only.
Do not delay the named mechanism to the second screen. Do not make Speaking, Certification, Advisory, and the book look like four unrelated brands. Do not let the homepage become the deepest explanation of anything.

A11 · Wave 2

Workshops

/workshops
Content job
Convert Sell the room the method, not a day out of the office.
Two buyers
An executive or L&D leader who owns a team's decision quality and has a standing forum where the tension shows up.
Critical framing
The Power of &™ is the Solution. The & Intelligence™ Framework is how you bring it to life, in three steps. A workshop is where a team runs those steps on a real decision. Do not mention Facilitator Certification: it is out of scope for this phase.
Section order
  1. Lead with the decision the team is stuck on, not the agenda. The workshop exists because a real tension is being forced into either/or.
  2. What the team leaves with: shared language, a named tension, and a tested next step.
  3. How the session runs, structured around Pause & Reflect, Find the &, Test & Learn.
  4. Who it is for: executive teams, boards, and standing leadership forums.
  5. Format and commitment: group size, half day or full day, in person or virtual, lead time.
  6. What comes with it: materials, the framework visuals, and a follow-up check-in.
  7. Proof and how to apply.
One CTA
Design a workshop. Not "inquire" — application signals selectivity and raises perceived value.
Do not call it training or an offsite. Do not sell an agenda: sell what the team can decide afterwards. Do not reference Facilitator Certification anywhere.

A16 · Wave 2 · Build before any additional article

The Capacity Audit

/capacity-audit
Content job
Convert Turn the Four Taxes from an explainer into a qualification engine.
What to write
  1. Intro block, 60–90 words. Hook: "Which tax is your organization paying?"
  2. Ten questions, ~2–3 per tax, written as recognizable executive situations. Four-point agreement scale.
  3. Four result profiles, one per dominant tax. Each 120–160 words: pattern, cost, and the one move most likely to change it.
  4. Result-page CTA that differs by profile but is always singular.
One CTA
Take the Capacity Audit on entry. Result page routes to relevant proof case or advisory conversation.
Do not write questions that flatter the reader into a good score. Do not use the word quiz.
A17 · Wave 3

Nurture email sequence

Five emails, triggered by chapter one download
Content job
Convert Walk a new subscriber down the master spine, one idea per email.
Length
Under 150 words per email. One insight, one link, one action. Two subject line variants each.
Sequence
  1. Overload. What happens to judgment when load outruns capacity. Links to /binary-trap.
  2. The Binary Trap. Why the decisive call is often the expensive one. Links to /binary-trap.
